AirSweep vs Vibrators

Silo flow aids compared

AirSweep vs Vibrators and Fluidisers

When silo vibrators, aeration pads and air cannons do not cure a blocked hopper

Industrial vibrators and fluidisers (aeration pads) are the most common flow aids fitted to silos, hoppers and bins. They are cheap to buy and simple to fit, and on the right material they work. But many sites end up with a vibrator that shakes the whole building while the hopper stays blocked, or aeration pads that hiss away while the material hangs up a metre further up the cone.

This page compares how vibrators, fluidisers, air cannons and AirSweep® work, where each one fits, and why they fail. IPT Engineering is the UK agent for AirSweep from Control Concepts, and much of what follows comes from their experience of more than 10,000 AirSweep systems, many fitted where vibrators or fluidisers had already been tried.

Already have a vibrator or aeration pads that are not doing the job? Tell us about your silo or hopper.

How Each Flow Aid Works

Shaking, aerating, blasting or sweeping

  • Industrial vibrators (silo, hopper and bin vibrators): an electric, pneumatic or hydraulic vibrator bolted to the vessel wall shakes the wall to break the material free. Impactors and air knockers are a variation that hit the wall like an automatic hammer.
  • Fluidisers and aeration pads (air pads, fluidising pads, bin aerators): rubber or fabric pads or discs mounted inside the vessel let low-pressure air seep into the material, combined with a little vibration of the pad, so a fine powder near the wall behaves more like a liquid.
  • Air cannons (air blasters): a large tank of compressed air is dumped through a nozzle to blast a hole through a heavy build-up.
  • AirSweep: nozzles fitted through the vessel wall fire short, high-pressure, high-volume 360-degree pulses of plant air along the inside of the wall in sequence. Each pulse lifts the material off the wall and sweeps it back into the flow, so arches and ratholes cannot form. Each nozzle clears roughly a 1.2 m (4 ft) radius.

Control Concepts sum up the difference between fluidisers and AirSweep as comparing a garden hose with a fire hose: both use air, but AirSweep delivers far more force and volume in each pulse.

Why Vibrators Fail

Compaction, noise and a fatigued vessel

  • They compact the material. Vibration packs damp, fine or cohesive material tighter. Instead of breaking the bridge, it makes the arch stronger. One food manufacturer told Control Concepts that their vibrators "practically turned the flax into concrete".
  • They leave residue. Wet or sticky material stays stuck to the walls, so someone still ends up on a ladder with a rod.
  • They damage the vessel. Continuous shaking fatigues welds, brackets and thin walls, and vibrators usually need channel stiffeners welded on to spread the load.
  • They are noisy. The noise travels through the structure and the whole building. Operators often switch them off, or only switch them on once the hopper is already blocked, which is too late.

Vibrators can work well on dry, free-flowing materials with an occasional hang-up. They are a poor choice for damp, sticky or fine materials, which are exactly the ones that bridge most.

Why Fluidisers and Aeration Pads Fail

Gentle air only suits light, dry powders

  • Light powders only. Low-pressure aeration works on fine, dry, free-flowing powders. Damp, sticky, stringy or heavy materials simply soak up the air or let it escape through a channel.
  • Small reach. Each pad only activates the material close to it, so large or tall cones need many pads, with more fittings, pipework and accessories.
  • Continuous air. Many fluidisers run continuously, so the total air and energy used can be higher than expected, even though each pad looks small.
  • Inside the vessel. Pads are mounted on the inside, where they block, tear or wear and can only be inspected by entering the vessel.
  • Flooding. Over-aerated fine powder can rush out uncontrollably like a liquid when it does let go.

Replacing a Vibrator or Aeration Pads with AirSweep

Usually without modifying the vessel

AirSweep nozzles are fitted through the vessel wall from outside, into steel, stainless, concrete, fibreglass or wooden vessels. Existing vibrators can usually be left in place and switched off once AirSweep is running, so there is no need to remove them first. A controller fires the nozzles in sequence, either on a timer or linked to the discharge.

With Control Concepts, we work out the number, size and position of the nozzles for your vessel and material, from a drawing or sketch, a few photos and the details in the form below.

Vibrator and Fluidiser Questions

Do silo vibrators work?

On dry, free-flowing materials with an occasional hang-up, often yes. On damp, sticky or fine materials they often compact the material and make bridging worse, and they fatigue the vessel and are noisy.

Why is my hopper still blocking with a vibrator fitted?

The vibrator is probably packing the material tighter rather than breaking the arch, especially if the material is damp, fine or has stood for a while. A flow aid that moves material along the wall without shaking the vessel, such as AirSweep, is usually the answer.

What is the difference between a fluidiser and an AirSweep?

Fluidisers or aeration pads let low-pressure air seep into the material near the pad and suit light, dry powders. AirSweep fires short, high-pressure 360-degree pulses along the wall, which also move wet, sticky, stringy and heavy materials.

Can AirSweep replace aeration pads or vibrators?

Usually, yes. AirSweep nozzles are fitted from outside the vessel, and existing vibrators can be left in place and switched off. We size the system with Control Concepts from a drawing, photos and details of the material.

How much compressed air does AirSweep need?

AirSweep runs on plant air at 80 to 100 psi (5.5 to 7 bar). It fires short intermittent pulses rather than running continuously, so the average air use is low. We work out the air demand for your vessel when we size the system.

Will AirSweep damage my silo?

No. AirSweep does not vibrate or hammer the vessel, so it can be used on thin-walled steel, fibreglass and other vessels that would be damaged by vibrators or air cannons.
