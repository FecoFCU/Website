AirSweep

Material flow systems for silos and hoppers

AirSweep®: Cure Blocked Silos and Hoppers

UK agent for Control Concepts USA

IPT Engineering is the UK agent for Control Concepts USA, the manufacturer of AirSweep®. You tell us the problem, and with Control Concepts we design an AirSweep system to cure it.

AirSweep is used for three kinds of problem:

  • Blocked material: bridging or ratholing that stops discharge altogether
  • Material not flowing: erratic or slow discharge, or material building up on the vessel walls
  • Batch cleaning: clearing a vessel between different materials or product runs, with less flushing and manual cleaning

How AirSweep Works

Compressed air or inert gas from the plant supply, at 80 to 100 psi (about 5.5 to 7 bar), is stored in a receiver close to the vessel. On a timed sequence, a high-flow valve releases a short, powerful pulse, typically a quarter of a second, through each AirSweep nozzle.

The nozzle directs the air in a 360-degree sweep along the inside of the vessel wall. This breaks the friction between the material and the wall, and lifts stalled material back into the flow. The nozzle then reseals immediately, so material cannot get back into it. One nozzle can activate material over a circle up to 2.4 m (8 ft) across.

The nozzles are pulsed one after another while the vessel discharges, or on demand, for example as a gate opens. Because each pulse is so short, air use is low: as little as 10 CFM (about 0.3 m³/min).

A typical system has an air receiver and filter, a compressed air header, isolation valves and flexible hoses to each nozzle, and a control box that sets the pulse sequence. Nozzles can be mounted on metal, concrete, fibreglass and wooden vessels, and are serviced from outside.

Proven Across Industries

AirSweep results from Control Concepts

AirSweep is used by more than half of S&P 500 companies. These Control Concepts installations show the kind of problems it cures, from heavy minerals to food-grade powders.

Bulk bag filling

Minerals processing

Problem: Alumina-based minerals, from fine powder to small pebbles, plugged the outlets of bins with 70 to 75 degree cones. Filling one bulk bag took an hour, and operators were hammering the bins.

AirSweep: Four VA-12 nozzles per bin, pulsed one at a time every 15 seconds while discharging, using under 8 CFM at 80 psi.

Filling time cut from one hour to two minutes. Plugging eliminated, and the system extended to 16 more bins.

Blender cleaning between batches

Food ingredients

Problem: Ribbon blenders were flushed with flour between custom spice and seasoning batches, a slow and labour-intensive clean-out.

AirSweep: Eight VA-12 Tri-Clamp nozzles, four on each side of the blender, about 0.3 m above the bottom.

62% less flushing flour and 42% less labour, saving around $200,000 in flush material alone.

Dry mix bins

Pet food manufacturing

Problem: Meat meal, ground grain and mineral blend at about 7% moisture bridged above the slide gates. Vibrators and pneumatic hammers had not worked, so operators climbed to the bins with sledgehammers.

AirSweep: Three VA-12 nozzles per bin, pulsed for a quarter of a second every 20 seconds for first-in, first-out flow. A VA-06 in stainless steel clears kibble fines during automated clean-outs.

Vibrators and hammers replaced, and more of the site's bins being converted to AirSweep.

Damp powder in lined chutes

Lithium processing

Problem: Lithium carbonate at 10 to 25% moisture stuck in PTFE-lined drop chutes, and hammers were used to keep it moving.

AirSweep: 24 VA-06 nozzles fitted with the Outside-In mounting coupling, so the lined chutes did not need welding or bolting.

The plant's filling target was reached, hammering stopped, and further systems have been ordered.

Moist sand in a batching bin

Ready-mix concrete

Problem: Damp sand bridged above the bin outlets, delaying truck filling. Vibrators and jogging the gates did not cure it.

AirSweep: One 2 inch VA-51 nozzle above the outlets, pulsed by the batch control each time the gate opens.

Sand flows on demand, trucks leave on schedule, and nobody climbs the ladder to poke it out.

Silo Bridging and Ratholing

Why bulk material stops flowing

Bridging (or arching) happens when material forms a stable arch over the outlet, so nothing discharges even though the vessel is full. Ratholing is when material flows only through a narrow channel above the outlet, leaving stagnant material packed around the walls.

Common causes include:

  • Moisture and sticky or cohesive materials
  • Material compacting under its own weight, or settling while the vessel stands
  • Fine powders, fibrous or irregularly shaped particles
  • A hopper angle or outlet that is too shallow or too small for the material
  • Build-up on the vessel walls that grows over time

Typical warning signs are erratic or stopped discharge, people hammering on the vessel, less usable capacity than the vessel should hold, and old material lingering in the vessel.

Ways to Fix a Blocked Silo

How AirSweep compares with other flow aids

Several types of flow aid are used to cure bridging and ratholing. The right choice depends on the material, the vessel and the problem.

Flow aidHow it worksPoints to consider
Air cannonsRelease a large blast of air at intervals to break up heavy build-ups, often in large vessels.Effective on heavy accumulations; can be noisy, and the blast is localised.
VibratorsShake the vessel wall to dislodge material.Simple to fit, but loud; repeated shaking can fatigue welds and metal, and some materials compact further, making the problem worse.
Fluidisers and aeration padsLow-pressure air through flexible discs or pads aerates the material near the wall.Suited to light, dry powders; each unit covers a small area, so many may be needed; disc material can wear and get into the product.
HammeringOperators strike the vessel to free the material.No equipment cost, but dents and damages the vessel, takes labour, and risks injury.
Live-bottom bin dischargersMechanically drive material out of the base of the vessel.Positive discharge, but usually a larger investment, with moving parts and changes to the vessel.
AirSweepShort, high-volume 360-degree air pulses sweep material off the wall and back into the flow.No vibration or wall stress; a quiet hiss rather than a loud blast; low air use; each nozzle covers up to 2.4 m; works on damp, sticky and heavy materials as well as powders; supports batch cleaning; 7-year warranty.

AirSweep can be a replacement for air cannons, fluidisers, vibrators and live-bottom dischargers. If you already have one of these and it is not solving the problem, tell us what is fitted.

AirSweep vs Vibrators

  • Vibrators shake the whole vessel from outside; AirSweep activates the material from inside, along the wall
  • No vibration, so no weld or metal fatigue on the vessel
  • A quick, quiet pulse instead of noise carried through the whole building
  • Does not compact the material, and clears the walls so build-up does not force shutdowns for cleaning
  • Fully clears each batch for first-in, first-out flow without cross-contamination

AirSweep vs Fluidisers

  • Fluidisers suit light powders such as flour; AirSweep also handles moist, sticky and heavy materials
  • One AirSweep nozzle covers up to 2.4 m (8 ft) of material, where fluidisers clear a small radius each
  • Only the nozzle tip touches the product: no rubber discs to wear and contaminate it
  • Low air use, and nothing to fit or maintain from inside the vessel
  • Quiet in operation, and USDA-accepted models are available for food

Comparisons are based on Control Concepts' published information.

AirSweep Questions

Will AirSweep damage my silo or hopper?

No. AirSweep does not vibrate the vessel, and it can be fitted to metal, concrete, fibreglass and wooden vessels.

What powers an AirSweep system?

Compressed air or gas from the plant supply at 80 to 100 psi (about 5.5 to 7 bar), stored in a receiver and released in short pulses by a high-flow valve on a timed sequence. AirSweep uses less plant air than fluidisers and air cannons.

Is AirSweep noisy?

No. Many flow aids are loud, but an AirSweep pulse makes a quiet hissing sound. It also removes the need for hammering on the vessel.

How is AirSweep maintained?

Each nozzle has one moving part and is serviced from outside the vessel; it can be inspected and removed without tools. Nozzles need inspecting about every six months, with parts replaced after around a million cycles, and rebuild kits are available.

How much compressed air does AirSweep use?

Very little, because each pulse lasts only a fraction of a second. A system can use as little as 10 CFM (about 0.3 m³/min). In one Control Concepts installation, four nozzles on a 200-ton bin used under 8 CFM at 80 psi.

What warranty does AirSweep have?

Control Concepts gives a 7-year warranty on the AirSweep unit.

Can AirSweep be fitted without welding?

Yes. The Outside-In mounting coupling attaches nozzles without welding or bolting, for example on lined chutes. Standard nozzles fit into a coupling welded or bolted to the vessel.

What can I see once it is installed?

Very little. Outside the vessel there is a valve mounted on the wall; inside, only the head of the nozzle, sitting flat against the vessel side.

Can AirSweep clean a vessel between batches?

Yes. It can be used to clear a vessel between different materials or product runs, reducing flushing and manual cleaning.

Is it suitable for food production or ATEX areas?

AirSweep is USDA-accepted, and Tri-Clamp models are available for hygienic applications. For ATEX areas, tell us the zone and we will confirm a suitable configuration with Control Concepts.

How is a system designed?

Send us details of the vessel, the material and the problem. With Control Concepts we work out the number and position of nozzles, the air supply and the controls, and propose a system.
