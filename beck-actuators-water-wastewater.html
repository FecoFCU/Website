Beck Electric Actuators for Water & Wastewater

Continuous modulation with no duty limits and no routine maintenance

Beck electric actuators are widely used in water and wastewater treatment plants in the USA, on filter, aeration, sludge and penstock duties where conventional actuators overheat, wear and need regular attention. IPT Engineering is the UK representative for Beck, and has supplied and supported Beck drives in UK power, steel, glass and process plants for many years.

Under AMP8, water companies are being asked to reduce equipment failure, move from reactive to proactive maintenance and lower whole-life cost. Beck’s design removes the parts that usually fail on a modulating actuator: there is no burn-out motor, no thermal overload, no torque switch, no worm gear and no battery.

Applications in Water & Wastewater Plants

Aeration control

Blower inlet vanes and air distribution header valves, modulating continuously to hold dissolved oxygen set points without overheating.

Filter control

Filter effluent (rate of flow) valves, backwash and wash water return valves, where steady flow control gives better filter performance.

Penstocks and sluice gates

A modified Group 11 for rising-stem duties, for inlet, storm and tank isolation penstocks and weir gates.

Sludge handling

Return and waste activated sludge (RAS and WAS) valves, including valves on extension bonnets in pits.

Digesters and hazardous areas

Digester valves, with ATEX versions for Zone 2 and Zone 22 areas.

Pump speed control

Scoop and adjustable speed fluid and magnetic couplings on pumps and fans, an area where IPT has long experience.

Treatment processes

Ozone, reverse osmosis and membrane valves, rotating scum pipes, interconnect and well station valves.

Sludge incineration

Natural gas, scrubber, combustion air, exhaust gas and fan dampers.

Beck Compared with Conventional Actuators

Typical conventional actuatorBeck actuator
DutyStarts limited to protect the motor from inrush heating; modulating duties often restricted to a start every few seconds100% continuous modulation with no duty-cycle or starts-per-hour limit
Motor protectionThermal overloads and torque switches, which can trip and take the valve out of controlNo-burnout motor that cannot overheat, even stalled; no thermal overloads or torque switches needed
GearingWorm gears add friction, heat, backlash and wearSpur gears in heat-treated alloy steel and ductile iron, essentially no wear-induced backlash
PositioningCoasting and overshoot limit small movesFull torque in under 50 ms, stops in 25 ms; steps as small as 0.1°
Manual operationDeclutch lever to engage the handwheelHandwheel always engaged, no declutch, safe with the motor running
MaintenancePeriodic inspection, with rebuilds every 5 to 8 years in harsh dutiesNo recommended periodic maintenance; 3 year warranty
Power lossPosition held by the gearing; batteries in some designsStays in place holding 200% of rated torque; optional ultra-capacitor backup unit, no batteries

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Beck actuator modulate continuously?

Yes. The Beck motor has starting and stall currents close to its running current, so it does not heat up with repeated starts. There is no duty-cycle or starts-per-hour limit, and it can track a 4–20 mA signal continuously.

What maintenance does a Beck actuator need?

Beck recommends no periodic maintenance. The motor has grease-sealed bearings, the spur gear train is lubricated for life and the position sensor is contactless.

Do Beck actuators need torque switches or thermal overloads?

No. The motor cannot overheat, even stalled, and the control module has configurable stall protection. Only normal short-circuit protection is needed.

Can Beck actuators be used on penstocks?

Yes. A modified Group 11 is available for rising-stem duties such as penstocks and sluice gates.

Are Beck actuators suitable for UK power supplies?

Yes. They run on 50 Hz, and an on-board transformer allows 240 V single-phase or 380/415 V three-phase supplies.
