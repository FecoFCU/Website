Obsolete Actuator Replacement

Re-engineering hydraulic and electric actuator applications

IPT Engineering replaces obsolete and unreliable hydraulic and electric actuators, re-engineering each application with modern electric actuation. Typical applications include fan vanes, dampers, valves, diverter gates and fluid coupling controls. Replacing hydraulic power packs and worn electric drives removes high-cost maintenance, process weaknesses and environmental hazards.

We look after the whole project, from the first enquiry and site survey through to supply, installation, commissioning and long-term support, using our own dedicated team.

Our experience covers power stations, glass works, energy from waste, steel works, refineries, gypsum works and limestone plants. We are the UK agent for Beck electric actuators, and use them across our projects. Every Beck drive is sold complete with a three year warranty.

What We Do

Re-engineering Existing Applications

We re-engineer existing applications using Beck electric actuators, including fan vanes, dampers, valves, diverter gates and fluid coupling controls. In many cases we have retrofit mounting plates to allow quick, drop-in upgrades of existing actuators.

Application Reviews & Site Surveys

We review the application and survey the site, so the right drive is selected for the duty from the start. Product demonstrations are also available.

Specification Writing

We can write the actuator specification for new installations, based on the application review.

Supply

We supply Beck electric actuators, and every Beck drive is sold complete with a three year warranty. If your existing actuator is obsolete or no longer supported, send us the nameplate details or photographs through Find a Part and we will help identify a replacement.

Installation & Commissioning

Our own dedicated team provides installation and commissioning assistance on all our drives, supported by the Beck Link Assist™ programme.

Training & Aftersales Support

Aftersales support is available 24/7, including troubleshooting assistance, spares support and field service engineers. We can also provide on-site training.

Applications & Experience

We have re-engineered a wide range of applications using Beck actuators, including:

Power Stations

At power stations we have replaced obsolete and unreliable hydraulic and electric actuators on fan vanes, dampers and valves, re-engineering each application to remove high-cost maintenance, process weaknesses and the environmental hazards associated with hydraulic power packs.

The upgrades improved process performance and system reliability, eliminated environmental hazards and gave the plants a maintenance-free solution. At one site, replacing the feed regulating valve actuators allowed a section of the system that had been out of service for many years to be recommissioned, making the process more cost-effective to operate.

Power station actuator installations, including original drives and their Beck replacements

  • Secondary air dampers

  • Boiler feed pump fluid coupling control

Glass Works

Glass furnaces run around the clock for years between rebuilds, so an actuator failure can quickly affect glass quality and production. IPT Engineering has supplied, installed, commissioned and supported Beck electric actuators on glass furnaces, forehearths and gas skids, replacing outdated drives on gas and combustion air valves and dampers.

We also supplied a Beck actuator as original equipment for a glass level probe more than 20 years ago, and it is still in service today.

Water & Wastewater Treatment

Under AMP8, water companies are being asked to reduce equipment failure, move to proactive maintenance and improve the resilience of critical assets. Continuous modulating duties on critical valves and dampers are a good fit for Beck actuators, which have no duty limits and no routine maintenance requirements.

We are happy to arrange discussions and product demonstrations for water companies and their contractors. The full case is set out on our Beck Electric Actuators page.
